CLEARLY: HEARING MATHS
Every formula and constant in packages/core, with the reason for it. Code: packages/core/src.
Constants live in constants.ts (each with a one-line source or reason); this file gives the
reasoning in full. Measured results: docs/sim-results.txt (generated).

0. HONESTY FIRST
Consumer headphones vary by roughly plus or minus 10 dB across frequency, which is as large as the
mild losses we measure, and a browser cannot know the real output level. Every result is
therefore "how this person heard these sounds with these headphones", never an absolute hearing
level. The profile carries a per-ear, per-band headphoneCompensation array (dB, default zeros) so
a correction table can be added later without a schema migration.

1. UNITS
Everything inside the estimator is absolute dBFS (decibels relative to digital full scale).
Results are stored in dBFS and relative to the calibration level Lc. Variable names end in Dbfs
or RelLc and the two are never mixed; conversion is only in estimator/levels.ts:
  valueRelLc = valueDbfs - Lc,   valueDbfs = valueRelLc + Lc.
Storing RelLc lets a later Refine re-seed correctly after the person recalibrates (new Lc).

2. LEVELS AND GRID (estimator/levels.ts)
  Lc          the person's comfortable calibration level, between -80 and -12 dBFS. Calibration
              stops at -12, so Lc is never louder than that.
  Ceiling     min(Lc + 12, -6) dBFS. At most 12 dB above a comfortable level, and never above
              -6 dBFS (the loudest tone peak allowed).
  Floor       -90 dBFS. Presented levels are clamped to [-90, ceiling].
  Grid        candidate thresholds on 1 dB steps from max(Lc - 70, -100) up to ceiling + 10.
              Mass below -90 means "heard even at the quietest level we can play"; mass above the
              ceiling means "no response at the loudest safe level". Each becomes a flag when at
              least 50% of the posterior lies there (EDGE_FLAG_MASS: "more likely than not").

3. TABLES (tables.ts)
3.1 Starter median loss (dB) at 250, 500, 1000, 2000, 4000, 8000 Hz. Approximates the median
    trends of ISO 7029 (age-related hearing threshold statistics):
      under 40   0  0  0  0  0  0
      40 to 49   0  0  1  2  6 12
      50 to 59   0  1  2  5 12 22
      60 to 69   1  3  5 10 20 32
      70 to 79   3  6  9 17 30 44
      80 plus    5  9 13 25 40 55
    3 and 6 kHz come from PCHIP interpolation (section 4). If the age question is skipped we
    assume 50 to 59, the middle of the people Clearly is for.
3.2 Normal-hearing offset R(f) in dB relative to 1 kHz, approximating the shape of ISO 226 (equal
    loudness, threshold curve) and ISO 389 (earphone reference thresholds):
      250 +9, 500 +3, 1000 0, 2000 -3, 3000 -4, 4000 -5, 6000 +4, 8000 +13.
    A normal ear needs more level at 250 and 8000 Hz and less around 3 to 4 kHz.

4. INTERPOLATION (pchip.ts)
Shape-preserving monotone cubic Hermite interpolation (PCHIP), Fritsch and Carlson (1980), on a
log2-frequency axis (octaves equally spaced, as on an audiogram). Tangents start as the average of
neighbouring secants, are zero where the data turn or are flat, and are pulled inside the
Fritsch-Carlson circle (alpha^2 + beta^2 <= 9). Result: on every interval the curve is monotone
between its two data values, so it never overshoots. A flat stretch stays exactly flat, and a
notch at 4 kHz does not ring. Outside the measured range the end value is held (no
extrapolation), so Express's unmeasured 250 Hz takes the 500 Hz value. Tested with flat, steep,
notched and 200 random shapes sampled densely.

5. ESTIMATOR (estimator/*.ts)
One Bayesian track per ear and frequency, a posterior over the grid, updated in log space.

5.1 Prior. Gaussian with mean
      Lc - 45 + R(f) + starterLoss(ageRange, f)   dBFS,
    clamped into the grid. 45 dB: comfortable listening sits about 40 to 50 dB above threshold
    for normal hearing, so a normal threshold is about 45 dB below Lc. Prior SD: TUNED to 13.5 dB
    (allowed range 12 to 18; see 5.9).

5.2 Likelihood. P(heard | L, theta) = gamma + (1 - gamma - lambda) * Phi((L - theta) / sigma),
    and 1 minus that for "not heard". gamma (guess rate) starts at 0.02, lambda (lapse rate) is
    0.04, sigma is 3 dB. sigma is deliberately a little wider than the simulated listener's true
    2 dB, so the model is not over-confident about slope. Phi is the standard normal CDF, computed
    from the Numerical Recipes erfcc approximation (fractional error under 1.2e-7).

5.3 Guess-rate update. gamma has a Beta(1, 49) prior (mean 0.02, worth 50 pseudo-trials). With n
    catch trials and fa false alarms the posterior mean is (1 + fa) / (50 + n), clamped to
    [0.01, 0.15]. It is recomputed after each catch trial once at least 3 exist, and every track's
    posterior is rebuilt from its prior and all its trials with the new gamma. The Beta prior
    stops one slip in three trials from tripling gamma, which the old raw-rate update did.

5.4 Next level. Posterior median plus uniform jitter in +/-2 dB, rounded to 0.5 dB, clamped to
    [-90, ceiling]. The median is the bisection point; the jitter stops the next level being
    predictable from the last answer.

5.5 Stop rule. A track stops when its posterior SD is at most stopSd after at least 2 trials, or
    at maxTrials. Express: stopSd TUNED to 5.5 dB, maxTrials 4. Refine: stopSd 3.5 dB, up to 8.

5.6 Information sharing. Just before a track's first trial its prior mean is shifted by
      0.6  x (posterior mean - population prior mean) of the nearest finished frequency in the
            same ear (equidistant neighbours on the log axis are averaged), plus
      0.45 x (posterior mean - population prior mean) of the finished track at the same frequency
            in the other ear.
    "Population prior mean" is the section 5.1 value before any sharing, so the shift measures
    how far this person differs from the population. Measuring against a neighbour's
    already-shifted prior would under-count that difference. The other-ear weight was TUNED from
    0.3 to 0.45. The weights matter less than the SD settings because tracks are interleaved at
    random, so early tracks have no finished neighbours.

5.7 Scheduling. Express: 500, 1000, 2000, 4000, 8000 Hz x 2 ears = 10 tracks, chosen at random
    among unfinished tracks, never the same track twice in a row (unless it is the only one
    left). Refine: all eight bands x 2 ears = 16 tracks. The 10 Express tracks start from their
    Express posterior (Gaussian, mean and SD, stored relative to Lc); the 6 new tracks start from
    the population prior shifted by information sharing from the finished Express tracks. Refine
    also continues the Express catch-trial history for the guess rate.

5.8 Catch trials. Silent trials: the cue shows, no tone plays, and a "heard" tap is a false alarm.
    They never touch thresholds; they only feed the guess rate. Target count:
    max(minimum, floor(tone trials / 5)), minimum 6 (Express) or 8 (Refine). Never the first trial
    and never two in a row. Placement: at each step, if catch trials are still owed, one is drawn
    with probability owed / (expected tone trials left + 1). It is forced when the number owed
    reaches the fewest tone trials that can remain (each open track needs at least one more, and
    at least 2 in total), which guarantees the minimum.

5.9 Output. Threshold = posterior mean; uncertainty = posterior SD; plus the two edge flags.

5.10 Tuning (M01 section 6 ladder). Stage 1 (maxTrials 4) passed. scripts/tune.ts searched
    stopSd {4, 4.5, 5, 5.5, 6} x prior SD {12, 13.5, 15, 16.5, 18} x same-ear weight {0.4, 0.6,
    0.8} x other-ear weight {0.15, 0.3, 0.45} x jitter {0, 1, 2, 3} on tuning seed 777 (300 runs
    each, 900 combinations, 53 passed). Five candidates were then rechecked on seeds 4242 and 9999
    (1000 runs each, Express and Refine). We chose stopSd 5.5, prior SD 13.5, weights 0.6 and 0.45,
    jitter 2: it passed on both seeds and keeps the specified jitter. Only then was the validation
    run made on the fixed seeds (20261109, 20261110), so it is not tuned to them. Margins are thin
    (95.35% against a 95% target), so any change to the estimator must rerun the suite.

5.11 Known limitation. With at most 4 trials a track can move only about 25 to 30 dB from its
    prior. For someone whose threshold at a band is far above the prior (or who hears nothing),
    Express stops below the truth with an SD that looks confident (about 6 dB). In the
    simulation, 71% of tracks whose truth is past the ceiling are flagged. Refine raises this to
    86%. This is listed in STATUS.txt as an open question; the fix (a heavier-tailed prior, or more
    trials when every answer agrees) changes the specified algorithm, so it needs the owner.

6. SIMULATED LISTENER (sim/listener.ts)
  Psychometric: true sigma 2 dB, lapse 4%, guess 2%; 10% of listeners guess 8 to 12%.
  Audiogram shapes (equal share): flat-normal (0 to 6 dB), gently sloping (rising to 15 to 40 dB
    at 8 kHz), steep high-frequency (35 to 60 dB from 4 kHz), notch at 4 kHz (20 to 45 dB deep),
    mild asymmetric (one ear 10 to 20 dB worse at high frequencies). Every band gets N(0, 3 dB)
    jitter, clamped to 0 to 60 dB; 1 kHz is clamped to 0 to 5 dB (near normal).
  Lc uniform in -45 to -20 dBFS.
  True threshold (dBFS) = Lc - 45 + calibration offset + R(f) + headphone colouring + loss, where
    the calibration offset is N(0, 5 dB) clamped to +/-12 (people set Lc by ear) and headphone
    colouring is N(0, 3 dB) per band, shared by both ears. These make the prior genuinely wrong,
    as it will be for real people.
  Age range: the starter row nearest the listener's 2, 4 and 8 kHz loss; 20% skip the question.
  Scoring: a track is scored for accuracy only if its true threshold lies inside the presentable
  range [-90, ceiling] (no estimator can measure beyond it). Censored tracks are counted
  separately, with how often the right edge flag was raised.

7. LOSS (hearing/loss.ts)
  loss(f) = max(0, (theta_f - R(f)) - (theta_ref - R(1000))),  theta in dBFS,
  theta_ref = the lower (better) of the two ears' 1 kHz thresholds.
  This assumes the better ear's 1 kHz hearing is near normal, which is typical of mild age-related
  loss but not universal. If both ears have loss at 1 kHz, all losses read too small. The UI says
  so (copy id hearingCheck.results.refNote, listed in REGULATORY_REVIEW.txt).
  headphoneCompensation (dB, per ear and band) is subtracted from each threshold first, including
  at 1 kHz, so it also moves the reference.
  Unmeasured bands: PCHIP on log frequency over the measured losses, uncertainty null, labelled
  "Estimated between measured points" (hearingCheck.results.estimatedBand).
  Uncertainty at a measured band is that track's posterior SD.

8. RELIABILITY (hearing/reliability.ts)
  n = catch trials, fa = false alarms (Refine counts its own and the Express ones).
  High    fa <= max(1, floor(0.15 n)) and every measured track's SD <= 6 dB.
  Medium  fa <= max(2, floor(0.30 n)) and at most two tracks have SD > 8 dB.
  Low     anything else.
  Counts with a floor (max(1, ...)) so one slip with only 6 catch trials does not sink the rating.
  Consequence: with n = 6 to 8, Low needs 3 or more false alarms. A listener who guesses 10% of
  the time has about a 2% chance of that (binomial), and for any n their expected count (0.1 n)
  stays below the Medium limit (0.30 n). The M01 target "at least 80% of high-guess listeners
  rated Low" is therefore unreachable under this rule. Measured: 1.9%. See STATUS.txt.

9. REFERRAL FLAGS (hearing/referral.ts)
  Average loss = mean over the eight bands. Reasons returned when:
    the two ears' averages differ by more than 20 dB;
    any single band differs by more than 30 dB between ears;
    either ear's average exceeds 40 dB.
  These are suggestions to see a hearing professional, never a diagnosis; the UI words them
  calmly.

10. GAIN (sound/gain.ts)
  k(loss) = smoothstep(2, 8, loss): 0 at or below 2 dB, 1 at or above 8 dB, 3t^2 - 2t^3 between.
    Replaces the old hard step at 5 dB, which made a 0.1 dB change in loss jump the gain.
  gain = k(loss) x min(cap, ratio x loss), then x strength, then min(24, ...).
  ratio by boost style: Gentle 0.33, Standard 0.5 (default), Strong 0.6. Half-gain is the classic
    starting point for mild loss; Gentle and Strong bracket it.
  cap default 18 dB, settable 12 to 24. Strength 0 to 1.5, default 1. Starter profiles are forced
    to Gentle with a 12 dB cap because they are a guess from age alone.
  Continuity: the steepest slope is about 2.7 dB of gain per dB of loss (Strong, strength 1.5,
    inside the knee), so a 0.1 dB change moves gain by under 0.3 dB. The test checks under 0.5 dB
    over every style, strength and cap.

11. SUMMED-BOOST CLAMP (sound/boostClamp.ts, sound/biquad.ts)
  Filters, as the graph will build them: personal EQ peaking Q 1.0 at 250 to 6000 Hz, highshelf
  at 8000 Hz; Speech peak 2500 Hz Q 1.0 (+3 dB); Voice focus 2000 Hz Q 0.7 (up to +6 dB at full
  strength); Ambient boost 2500 Hz Q 0.8 (0 to 6 dB).
  Magnitudes use the Web Audio API 1.1 definitions (section 1.13.5, read 2026-11-09): A = 10^(G/40),
  w0 = 2 pi f0 / Fs, peaking alpha = sin(w0) / (2Q) with linear Q, highshelf alpha =
  sin(w0)/2 x sqrt(2) (S = 1, Q ignored), and the cookbook b and a coefficients. Fs = 48 kHz.
  The combined response is the sum in dB of every filter's magnitude on a log grid from 20 Hz to
  20 kHz, 48 points per octave. Voice focus acts on the mid channel, not on each ear, so adding it
  to each ear's EQ over-estimates slightly. That errs on the safe side.
  If the peak exceeds +24 dB, the extras (Speech, Voice focus, Ambient) are scaled by the largest
  factor in [0, 1] that fits (bisection, 40 steps). If even zero extras is too much, the EQ is
  scaled the same way. The EQ goes last because it is the part fitted to the person.
  FINDING for the audio milestone: neighbouring Q 1.0 peaks overlap strongly. Asking for 10 dB in
  every band produces a 28 dB peak, and a typical sloping prescription peaks at about 2x its band
  gains. Using the band gains directly as filter gains would therefore overshoot the intended
  curve and trigger this clamp often. The audio milestone should fit filter gains to the target
  curve. See STATUS.txt.

12. SETTINGS PRECEDENCE (sound/precedence.ts, sound/presets.ts)
  Lowest to highest: preset values; the person's manual changes (remembered per profile); Music
  forces cleanup and Voice focus to 0; this session's changes (only cleanup and Voice focus can be
  turned back on in Music); Night mode (from the Night preset or a manual change) replaces the
  compressor stage; Ambient boost adds its own 2500 Hz peak.
  Presets:
    Movie   the defaults: strength 1, compressor -24 dBFS / knee 12 / 4:1 / 10 ms / 250 ms,
            cleanup 0, Voice focus 0 (cleanup needs a download the person starts).
    Speech  +3 dB peak at 2500 Hz Q 1.0, ratio 3, Voice focus 50%, cleanup 40%.
    Music   EQ gains x 0.8, ratio 2, threshold -18 dBFS, knee 20 dB, cleanup and Voice focus 0.
    Night   Night mode Medium, strength 0.8.

13. TIMING AND DURATIONS (timing.ts)
  Cue ring 200 ms before onset. Tone 350 ms, warble with 5 Hz FM at +/-5%, 20 ms raised-cosine
  ramps, hard-panned, peak within [-90, -6] dBFS. Response window 1000 ms from onset. After "heard"
  the next trial may start 300 ms after the tap; after "not heard" at the window's end. Then a
  random gap of 250 to 450 ms. Slower pace multiplies the window, the post-tap wait and the gaps
  by 1.5, and turns on automatically for 70 to 79 and 80 plus.
  Mean tap time for duration estimates: 450 ms after onset (typical simple reaction time to
  near-threshold tones in people over 50). ASSUMPTION, to be replaced by measured data.
  expectedDurationSeconds(method, pace) = 4 s calibration + tone trials x (heard share x heard
  trial + rest x missed trial) + catch trials x missed trial, using the mean counts and heard
  share from the simulation (src/generated/simStats.ts, checked against a fresh run by the
  suite), rounded up to the nearest 10 s. Starter is a fixed 5 s. Currently: Express 60 s (80 s
  slower), Refine 100 s (130 s slower). No UI string may state a duration any other way.

14. RANDOMNESS (rng.ts)
  All randomness goes through an injectable seeded Rng: sfc32 (PractRand-tested small fast
  counter generator) seeded through splitmix32. Box-Muller for normals.

15. LISTENING HEALTH (M08)

15.1 Speech-shaped noise
Target: ANSI S3.5-1997 (R2017) Table 3, standard speech spectrum level, normal vocal effort, one-
third-octave centres 160 Hz to 8 kHz (dB, spectrum level per Hz): 32.41 34.48 34.75 33.98 34.59
34.27 32.06 28.30 25.01 23.00 20.15 17.32 13.18 11.55 9.33 5.31 2.59 1.13. Values as reproduced
by the MoSQITo implementation of the standard (read 2026-11-09). 125 Hz is outside the table and
taken equal to 160 Hz (flat region), an extrapolation.
White noise has a flat spectrum level, so the filter's gain follows these values up to a
constant. IIR approximation: low shelf 772.8 Hz +10.7 dB, peaking 468.2 Hz +4.66 dB Q 0.396,
peaking 2494.6 Hz -3.78 dB Q 1.16, high shelf 4174.8 Hz -22.85 dB, low-pass 12211 Hz Q 2.378 dB
(Web Audio formulas, lowpass Q in dB). Fitted by packages/core/scripts/fit-ltass.ts (random search
minimising the largest deviation after removing the mean). Fit error: at most 0.72 dB from 125 Hz
to 8 kHz; the unit test requires 2 dB.
The noise is scaled so its RMS is Lc - 15 dBFS, never above -12 dBFS; the filtered noise's RMS is
measured with a 2 s offline render rather than derived.

15.2 Digits in noise
Triplets of three different digits, 300 ms apart, noise from 0.5 s before to 0.5 s after. Diotic.
Speech (digits at -20 dBFS RMS after trimming and equalising, all within 0.5 dB) is played at
noise + SNR. Start at 0 dB SNR; all three right: 2 dB harder; otherwise 2 dB easier; clamped to
[-20, +10] dB. 12 triplets. SRT = mean of the SNRs of triplets 5 to 12 and the SNR the 13th
would have had. "Not sure" counts as wrong. Indicative categories, approximations that need
validation: Good at or below -8 dB; Some difficulty above -8 to -4 dB; Considerable difficulty
above -4 dB.

15.3 Safe-listening tracker
The calibration tone peaks at Lc dBFS; a sine's RMS is 3 dB below its peak (20 log10(sqrt 2) =
3.01 dB), so the reference RMS is Lc - 3 dBFS. The final output's stereo power mean is measured
over each report interval (50 ms), and readings are pooled into one-second means. Comfortable:
below the reference; Loud: reference to 6 dB above; Very loud: more than 6 dB above. Output below
-70 dBFS is not counted. The Hearing Check counts each tone at its peak minus 3 dB for its length.
Only minutes per local day and zone are stored. This is relative to the person's own level, not a
sound-pressure measurement; changing the computer's volume changes the truth behind it.

16. THE AUDIO PIPELINE (M02; packages/audio, constants.ts)
16.1 Engine. 48 kHz throughout (ENGINE_SAMPLE_RATE_HZ); files at other rates are resampled first.
  Every parameter change uses setTargetAtTime: Original | Enhanced and other switches with a
  30 ms time constant (about 150 ms to settle), sliders with 50 ms. Reason: no clicks (RULES).
16.2 Chain (buildProcessingGraph.ts). Upmix to stereo and the input meter; Background cleanup
  (section 17.3); Voice focus; the per-ear EQ (core section 10, after the summed-boost clamp of
  section 11) with the Speech and Ambient peaks; -3 dB pre-attenuation; the compressor (or Night
  mode, section 17); the program limiter; the Loudest-sound limiter; the wet gain. A delay-matched
  dry path runs alongside for Original. Wet and dry are summed, then the master gain, then last on
  every path the Safety limiter. Program limiter: threshold -3 dBFS, knee 0, ratio 20, attack 3 ms, release
  100 ms. Compressor "off" (Simulate my hearing): ratio 1 at 0 dBFS, kept in place so the latency
  never changes.
16.3 Safety limiter. Fixed ceiling -3 dBFS, true-peak style: 4 x oversampling (16 taps per phase,
  Kaiser beta 8), 2 ms look-ahead, 100 ms release, ceiling changes smoothed with 50 ms. It is the
  last processing node on every path and cannot be bypassed. Reason: no setting, file or bug may
  play louder than this (RULES defence in depth).
16.4 Overload guard. Any run of samples above -1 dBFS (gaps under 25 ms keep a run going: a
  20 Hz wave peaks every 25 ms) that lasts 200 ms fades the master to silence over 100 ms and tells
  the person; sound comes back (over 100 ms) only when they choose to resume.
16.5 Voice focus. Mid/side: the mid (centre, where dialogue usually sits) rises by up to +4 dB and
  the side falls by up to -6 dB, scaled by the slider. Core's clamp is told the actual +4 dB.
  Mono detection: side more than 60 dB under mid for 2 s (100 ms windows; windows quieter than
  -80 dBFS count for neither) treats the file as mono, where mid/side does nothing useful.
16.6 Simulate my hearing. The inverse of the profile's loss as cuts: at most 24 dB per filter and
  40 dB in total at any frequency.
16.7 Calibration ramp. 1 kHz tone from -60 dBFS rising 1.5 dB every 100 ms (each step ramped over
  5 ms); the person's "comfortable" tap fixes Lc. Tones are scheduled 50 ms ahead on the audio
  clock.
16.8 Offline renderer (exports). 120 s segments with 5 s of pre-roll each (so slow stages such as
  the rider settle) and 10 ms crossfades at the joins, so a long file renders exactly as it plays.
  WAV: 16-bit PCM with TPDF dither. MP3: 128, 192 (default), 256 or 320 kbps constant bitrate.
16.9 Video export (M11). The same render, then AAC at 192 kbps (FFmpeg's native encoder); the video
  is copied bit for bit. Tested: audio within 50 ms of the source length and 0.5 dB of the WAV
  render's level.
16.10 Meters. Readings every 50 ms; loudness as EBU R 128 momentary (400 ms windows, 100 ms
  blocks), K-weighted with the BS.1770 filters at 48 kHz.

17. NIGHT MODE AND BACKGROUND CLEANUP (M06)
17.1 Night mode rider. A slow loudness rider before the compressor: BS.1770 K-weighted loudness
  over a 3 s window in 100 ms blocks; gain rises at most 3 dB per second and falls at most 6 dB
  per second (it turns sudden loud scenes down faster than it brings quiet ones up); blocks
  below -60 LUFS are gated (silence never pumps the gain).
  Levels (target LUFS, largest boost, largest cut):
    Light  -24, +6 dB, -12 dB
    Medium -26, +9 dB, -12 dB
    Strong -28, +12 dB, -15 dB
17.2 Night mode compressor (after the rider; threshold 6 dB under the target, so it only catches
  peaks the slow rider lets through):
    Light  threshold -30 dBFS, knee 10 dB, ratio 3, attack 10 ms, release 250 ms
    Medium threshold -32 dBFS, knee 8 dB, ratio 5, attack 6 ms, release 200 ms
    Strong threshold -34 dBFS, knee 6 dB, ratio 8, attack 3 ms, release 150 ms
  Tested: a 30 dB swing shrinks more at each level, and the output stays inside the safety limits.
17.3 Background cleanup. RNNoise (BSD-3-Clause) on 480-sample frames (10 ms at 48 kHz) per
  channel, samples scaled by 32768 for the network. Total added delay is a fixed 480 + 480
  samples (algorithm plus ring buffer), the same at any strength and when the module is not
  loaded, so turning cleanup on or off never shifts the picture against the sound. Strength is a
  linear wet/dry mix, smoothed per sample. Tested: 0 percent nulls against the dry path; 100 percent lowers
  steady noise by 10 dB or more.

18. QUIET LAB (M09)
18.1 Hum Filter. Detection: 8192-point spectrum every 500 ms; search +-2 Hz around 50 and 60 Hz;
  a peak must stand 12 dB above its surroundings, move less than 0.5 Hz for 3 s, and is held 2 s
  through gaps (released after 6 s without it). The fundamental and the next five harmonics each
  get two notches (Q 30) stagger-tuned 0.12 percent either side, at most 12 notches: one
  notch removes 25 dB only within about +-0.04 Hz, closer than the spectrum can estimate; the pair
  keeps more than 40 dB across +-0.1 Hz of error. The estimate follows changes at 0.3 per update.
  Notches add no latency. Tested: hum at 49.5 to 60.5 Hz cut by 25 dB or more, speech within 1 dB.
18.2 Auto Boost. Room level = microphone RMS (dBFS), smoothed with a 2 s attack and 5 s release,
  with a 3 dB backlash. From -55 dBFS (quiet) to -30 dBFS (loud) the Ambient boost rises linearly
  from 0 to +6 dB and the compressor threshold falls from 0 to 6 dB; sensitivity moves both
  thresholds 5 dB (High -60 and -35, Low -50 and -25). Feedback guard: Pearson correlation of the
  output and microphone envelopes over 10 s above 0.6 for 5 s means the microphone hears the
  speakers, and the boost freezes. The boost passes through the summed-boost clamp (section 11),
  so with a strong profile it may add less than +6 dB (open question 16). The microphone never
  hears the ear: this is a guide, not a measurement.
18.3 Calm Sounds. Generated on the device: white (xorshift32), pink (Kellet's filter), brown
  (integrated white with a 20 Hz leak and a 5 Hz DC blocker), rain (500 Hz to 8 kHz band plus
  droplets), ocean (0.1 Hz swell), and "matched to my ringing" (a one-octave band around the
  matched pitch). Each is normalised to -20 dBFS RMS (measured over 20 s). Volume is hard-capped
  inside the graph at Lc - 10 dB, or -24 dBFS without a calibration; no slider value can pass the
  cap. Pitch match: third-octave steps from 250 Hz to 12.5 kHz, starting at 6 kHz. The sleep
  timer fades out over 60 s.

19. COMPUTER SPEAKERS (Hearing Check)
  Speakers reach both ears, so a speakers check measures both ears together: one track per
  frequency (Express 5, Refine 8), every tone at the requested peak in both channels (identical,
  as the calibration ramp), and the result copied to both ears (mirrorEars). Thresholds stay
  relative to Lc as with headphones. What changes is the acoustic path: the room, the distance
  and the speakers' own response all add to the result, so it is a coarser guide than a headphone
  check, and differences between the ears cannot be seen.
