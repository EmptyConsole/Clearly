CLEARLY: DESIGN RESEARCH
Milestone M00. Plain text, tracked. Last updated 2026-11-09.

HOW THIS WAS DONE
  Every reference below was opened live with Playwright (Chromium 158, headless) on 2026-11-09 at
  1440, 1024 and 390 px wide. The tool is scripts/research/measure.ts. Screenshots (viewport and
  full page) are saved under research/<slug>/ and are gitignored, never committed. Raw measurements
  are in research/measurements.json (also gitignored); the values below are copied from it.

  Measured per page: computed font family, size, weight, line height and tracking for body, p, h1,
  h2, h3; the most frequent font sizes, spacing values (padding, margin, gap), text and background
  colours (backgrounds weighted by area), corner radii, shadows, borders, transition durations and
  easings, max-widths; paragraph line length in characters (paragraph width divided by the average
  glyph width of a sample sentence in that font); heights of buttons and inputs; the focus style
  of the first focusable element.

  Limits, stated honestly: values come from the first screen of each home page (or one article),
  not a whole site. Cookie banners were left in place and appear in some screenshots. Measured
  button heights include links styled as buttons, so a few are nav links rather than primary
  buttons. Acclaim sources were also opened with Playwright on 2026-11-09 and the quoted claim was
  found on the page.

  Principle: learn proportions and principles, never copy. No logo, copy, illustration,
  photography, proprietary font or distinctive layout from any reference is used in Clearly.

PATTERNS ACROSS THE REFERENCES
  Body size. The three accessibility systems all settle on 19 px body text (GOV.UK, NHS.UK) or
  larger than 16 px. Things and Headspace use 18 px. Product sites (Linear, Stripe, Flighty) sit at
  15-16 px with smaller secondary text, which our users cannot rely on.
  Leading. 1.32 (GOV.UK) to 1.47 (NHS.UK, Apple). Our rule is 1.6 for body, which is looser than all
  of them; it suits 19 px Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, whose large x-height reads as bigger text.
  Line length. The best-read pages cap paragraphs at 70-76 characters (GOV.UK 71, Things 71,
  Guardian article 76).
  Spacing. Every well-made reference uses multiples of 4 or 8 (USWDS, Stripe, NHS.UK, Pudding most
  clearly). None use odd steps in their main rhythm.
  Radius. Accessibility systems use 0-4 px; calm consumer products 6-18 px; marketing pages use
  pills. No well-made reference uses one large radius for everything.
  Elevation. GOV.UK and NHS.UK use a solid darker bottom edge on buttons instead of blur; Things and
  Flighty use one or two very faint layered shadows; Stripe's soft wide shadows are the most
  decorative. Most surfaces are separated by a background tint, not a shadow.
  Motion. 100-200 ms everywhere except Apple (240-320 ms) and Stripe (up to 800 ms on decorative
  elements). GOV.UK and NHS.UK have no transitions at all.
  Focus. The accessibility systems use the thickest, most distinct focus: GOV.UK a 3 px outline
  plus a 2 px dark underline, NHS.UK 4 px plus a 4 px offset, USWDS 4 px. Product sites mostly leave the browser default (1 px).
  Targets. NHS.UK primary buttons are 60 px tall and GOV.UK inputs 50 px; most product-site buttons
  are 32-44 px, below our 48 px minimum.

TOKEN DECISIONS AND THE REFERENCES BEHIND THEM
  Text family: Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (SIL OFL 1.1), self-hosted, variable 200-800. Designed for
  the Braille Institute to keep easily confused letters (I l 1, O 0, b d p q) distinct, which serves
  readers with low vision. One family for headings and text: GOV.UK, NHS.UK and Linear show that one
  family with clear size and weight steps gives hierarchy without a display face, and it saves a
  font download. No display family is used. System-font fallback stack in tokens.json.
  Base size 19 px (default was 18): matches GOV.UK and NHS.UK, the two references written for the
  widest public. The smallest size token is 18 px, so nothing in the interface goes below the rule.
  Modular scale: ratio 1.25 (major third) from 19 px, rounded to whole pixels:
  18 (floor), 19, 24, 30, 37, 46, 58. GOV.UK's 19 to 24 to 36 to 48 steps are close to 1.25-1.33.
  Font sizes compile to rem so the browser's text-size setting scales them.
  Line heights: body 1.6 (rule), headings 1.2 (GOV.UK, NHS.UK sit at 1.15-1.3), tight 1.1 for the
  largest display size only, UI text 1.3.
  Tracking: headings -0.01 rem, a light touch of the tightening seen on Linear (-0.022 em), Stripe
  (-0.02 em) and Apple. Body text is never tightened; Atkinson is drawn with open spacing on purpose.
  Weights: 400, 500, 600, 700. NHS.UK uses 600 for headings and Linear uses in-between weights; we
  keep 700 for page headings and 600 for labels and table headers.
  Measure: 38 rem (608 px at 16 px root), about 68-72 characters of 19 px Atkinson. GOV.UK 640 px,
  Guardian 620 px.
  Spacing: 8 px base: 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128, plus 4 px for icon-to-label gaps only (USWDS,
  Stripe and NHS.UK all show this half step).
  Radius: replaced the single 12 px default with roles: control 8 px (buttons, inputs, toggles),
  surface 12 px (cards, panels), pill only for segmented toggles and the round test button, none
  for full-bleed bands. Between NHS.UK's 4 px and Things' 6-18 px; avoids the one-radius-everywhere
  pattern on the banned list.
  Borders: hairline 1 px for decorative dividers, 2 px for control boundaries (GOV.UK and NHS.UK
  draw inputs with 2 px borders so they read as fields), 4 px emphasis for selected state or the
  left rule of an inset notice (GOV.UK inset text).
  Elevation: two levels only. Raised: 0 1 2 at 8 percent, always with a hairline border (Things,
  Flighty). Overlay: 0 8 24 at 16 percent, dialogs and popovers only. No glow. A darker bottom edge
  on buttons (GOV.UK, NHS.UK) will be tried when the button component is built (later milestone);
  it is a component decision, not a token.
  Motion: fast 100 ms, base 160 ms, slow 200 ms, nothing longer except the visual
  Original | Enhanced crossfade at 400 ms. Easing "standard" is cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.46, 0.45, 0.94),
  the quad ease-out measured on Linear; "inout" is Apple's (0.4, 0, 0.6, 1). No spring or bounce.
  Reduced motion makes every transition instant.
  Colour: one primary (#0F5E5A, a deep teal from the starting defaults) plus warm neutrals. The
  off-white page (#FAFAF7) with white raised surfaces follows the tinted-band separation seen on
  NHS.UK (#F0F4F5 behind white) and Apple (#F5F5F7). Warm rather than cold greys follow Headspace.
  Warning was darkened from the default #B45309 to #A44B06 because #B45309 measured 4.39:1 on the
  sunken surface, below 4.5:1.
  Ear colours: right ear red (#C0392B light), left ear blue (#1E5AA8 light), the audiology
  convention, with circle and cross shapes as well. Checked under simulated protanopia,
  deuteranopia and tritanopia (Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes 2009 matrices, severity 1.0,
  https://www.inf.ufrgs.br/~oliveira/pubs_files/CVD_Simulation/CVD_Simulation.html, opened
  2026-11-09): the smallest CIE76 difference is 55 (dark theme, protanopia), far above the 20 we
  require.
  Focus ring: 3 px solid, 2 px offset, so the ring sits against the page colour rather than the
  control. Light theme uses near-black (#1B1B1A, 16.5:1 on the page); dark theme uses a warm yellow
  (#FFD54A, 13.1:1), chosen because a warm yellow stands apart from every other colour in the
  dark palette; high contrast uses black.
  Targets: minimum 48 px, input height 56 px (between GOV.UK 50 px and NHS.UK's 60 px buttons), test
  button 160 px.
  Breakpoints: tablet 768, desktop 1024 (fixed by the navigation spec), wide 1280. Content width
  1120 px, gutters 16 px on phones and 32 px from tablet up.
  Themes: light (default), dark (system dark scheme), high contrast (in-app switch or the system's
  increased-contrast setting): pure black on white, black control borders, darker semantic colours.
