Interpretations
- Catch-trial placement: random with probability owed / (expected tone trials left + 1), forced
  when the number owed reaches the fewest tone trials that can remain. This guarantees the
  minimum while never placing a catch first or two in a row. A track may repeat back to back only
  when it is the last one open.
- Edge flags are raised when at least 50% of the posterior lies past the floor or ceiling.
- Accuracy is scored only on tracks whose truth is inside [-90 dBFS, ceiling]; censored tracks are
  reported separately with their flag rate (Express 71%, Refine 86%).
- Starter audiograms store reliability null (they are not measured); the schema enforces null
  exactly when method is starter. Profiles allow calibration null for starter profiles.
- Audiograms also store each measured band's threshold (dBFS, RelLc, SD), so a later Refine can
  seed from a stored profile.
- Average loss for referral flags is the mean over all eight bands.
- Night mode has levels off, low, medium, high (the Night preset sets Medium). Its rider settings
  belong to the audio milestone; core only resolves the level.
- Voice focus is modelled for the clamp as a 2000 Hz peak, Q 0.7, up to +6 dB at full strength
  (constants.ts). The audio milestone must build the same filter from those constants. Ambient
  boost range 0 to 6 dB.
- Movie (the defaults) has cleanup 0 and Voice focus 0, because Background cleanup needs a
  download the person must start.
- Uncertainty at a measured band is the track's posterior SD (the section 3 output), not combined
  with the 1 kHz reference's SD.
- Unmeasured bands outside the measured range (250 Hz after Express) take the nearest measured
  value; PCHIP does not extrapolate.
- Duration estimates use the mean simulated trial counts and a 450 ms mean tap time (an
  assumption until real data exists). Express is "about 60 seconds" (80 at slower pace).
- Property tests for the clamp use the seeded Rng instead of adding fast-check (no new dependency).
- Schema fixtures are TypeScript modules (src/schemas/fixtures.ts) rather than JSON files, so core
  needs no Node types in its source tsconfig.

M02 (2026-11-09): packages/audio, graph, limiter, tone generator, offline renderer, encoders

Conflicts between the M02 prompt and RULES.md or physics (RULES.md wins; each recorded here)
- DRY-PATH DELAY. The prompt says the dry DelayNode is "exactly latencySamples (the cleanup slot
  plus both limiter lookaheads)". Both paths pass through the Safety limiter after they are summed,
  so the Safety limiter's lookahead delays wet and dry equally; including it in the dry delay would
  misalign them by 96 samples and comb-filter the crossfade. Followed RULES.md ("a DelayNode that
  exactly matches the wet path's total latency"): dry delay = wet latency (1632 samples);
  latencySamples = wet + Safety limiter = input-to-output latency (1728). The null test and an
  impulse in both modes confirm it.
- COMPRESSOR LATENCY. The prompt's latency sum lists only the cleanup slot and the limiter
  lookaheads, but Chromium's DynamicsCompressorNode delays by 288 samples (6 ms) at 48 kHz,
  measured 2026-11-09 as a pure delay (residual -151 dB against a delayed copy, at ratio 1 and at
  the RULES.md settings). The chain has two of them (compressor stage, program limiter). The Web
  Audio spec does not fix this delay, so buildProcessingGraph measures it once per sample rate with
  an impulse in an OfflineAudioContext and includes it in the budget. M06's Night-mode stage must
  have the same latency as the normal stage (CompressorStage.latencySamples).
- VOICE FOCUS RANGE. Core (M01) models Voice focus as up to +6 dB (VOICE_FOCUS_MAX_DB) and says the
  audio milestone must build that filter; the M02 prompt says mid 0 to +4 dB and side 0 to -6 dB.
  Followed the M02 prompt (the newer, more specific instruction). The clamp is told the boost the
  graph actually applies (4 dB x amount), so core and the graph cannot disagree. Core's
  VOICE_FOCUS_MAX_DB is now unused by the graph; the owner may want it changed to 4 (STATUS).
- CROSSFADE AT SEGMENT JOINS. The prompt asks for a 10 ms equal-power crossfade. The two segments
  carry the same audio in the overlap (same graph, warmed up by the pre-roll), and a true
  equal-power fade (sin, cos) of identical signals swells by up to +3 dB mid-fade, which would
  break the prompt's own requirement that segmented output match a single pass within -60 dB. Used
  the amplitude-complementary raised cosine (weights sin^2 and cos^2, which sum to 1): the
  equal-power shape on a power scale, exact for correlated audio. Measured residual: -348 dB.

Graph
- buildProcessingGraph is async: the worklet module (which holds the Safety limiter) and the
  compressor-latency measurement are awaited before any node exists. If the module cannot load, the
  build rejects and nothing is connected to the destination; there is no option to omit the
  Safety limiter. Topology is recorded by a Wiring helper (Web Audio cannot list connections) and
  tested: the only edge into the destination is from the Safety limiter in every mode.
- Hum filter: six stages, each a BiquadFilter "notch" crossfaded (30 ms) with its own bypass gain.
  A notch filter cannot be made flat by its parameters, so "off" is the bypass exactly
  (x * 1 + notch(x) * 0); the null test shows a flat response.
- Background cleanup slot: CLEANUP_LATENCY_SAMPLES = 960 (derivation in packages/audio
  src/constants.ts): 480 for RNNoise's overlap-add synthesis (ASSUMPTION, to verify in M06 with an
  impulse) plus a 480-sample ring-buffer prime, which works for any render quantum size (Chromium
  158 supports a configurable render quantum: 'renderQuantumSize' in AudioContext is true).
- Exact integer delays: AudioParam values are float32, so k / sampleRate is rounded up to the next
  float32 so a DelayNode never interpolates between two samples. Measured delay equals the budget.
- Simulate my hearing: cut = -min(loss, 24) dB per filter, and the cascade is held to at most 40 dB
  of total cut (all cuts scaled together by bisection on core's biquad formulas). Biquad shelves
  and peaks only approximate the inverse of a loss curve; documented in params.ts. Compression is
  off in Simulate by setting the compressor stage to ratio 1, threshold 0 dBFS (the node stays, so
  latency is unchanged; at ratio 1 Web Audio's makeup gain is 0 dB).
- Original keeps the Enhanced plan on the silent wet path so switching is a pure crossfade.
- Night mode: the Night-mode rider is M06. When settings request it, the normal compressor runs
  with core's default parameters and ProcessingGraph.capabilities.nightMode is false so the UI can
  say so plainly. Background cleanup likewise reports false until M06.
- setBlend(0..1) added: the Original | Enhanced crossfade position (wet = x, dry = 1 - x). setMode
  sets 0 or 1. The null test uses it at 25, 50 and 75 percent.
- Mono: an input-monitor worklet on Tap A applies the prompt's rule (RMS of L-R below -60 dB of L+R
  for 2 s, 100 ms windows; silence neither confirms nor breaks it). In mono the side cut goes to
  0 dB so only the mid boost applies.
- Overload guard: the output-monitor worklet watches the Safety limiter's output; a run continues
  across gaps under 25 ms (a 20 Hz waveform dips below the threshold twice per cycle). On trip the
  main thread ramps the master gain (before the Safety limiter) to 0 over 100 ms
  (cancelAndHoldAtTime when present, else cancelScheduledValues plus setValueAtTime) and emits
  'overload'; resumeAfterOverload() fades back and re-arms. Because the Safety limiter holds the
  output at -3 dBFS, a real -1 dBFS overload cannot be produced through the graph, so GraphIo has a
  test hook, overloadThresholdDbfs, which can only lower the threshold (it is clamped to at most
  -1 dBFS). Tests: forced (threshold -6, Original mode, full-scale square) fires once offline and
  silences a live AudioContext 0.24 s after onset; normal programme never fires.
- Taps: analyser, loudness (BS.1770 K-weighting, momentary 400 ms, coefficients from the libebur128
  analogue prototypes, checked against the BS.1770 48 kHz table), safe-listening tracker (the same
  readings with their duration), compression reduction (compressor stage, program limiter, both
  worklet limiters), captions Tap A.
- Web Audio's DynamicsCompressorNode applies automatic makeup gain (W3C spec; measured in
  Chromium: +8.2 dB at -24 dBFS, knee 12, 4:1 for quiet input; +1.7 dB for the program limiter at
  -3 dBFS, 20:1). Not compensated, because RULES.md specifies only the compressor parameters. This
  makes Enhanced louder than Original for quiet passages (owner question in STATUS).
- Band gains are core's earGainsDb output used directly as filter gains, then clampSummedBoost per
  ear (as the prompt requires: "never computed here"). STATUS open question 3 (fitting filter gains
  to the target curve because Q 1.0 peaks overlap) is still open and unchanged by this milestone.
  Voice focus is one stage shared by both ears, so it takes the stricter ear's clamp scale.

Lookahead limiter (clearly-limiter worklet; DSP in src/dsp/limiterCore.ts, unit-tested in Node)
- 4x polyphase FIR interpolation: 16 taps per branch, Kaiser-windowed sinc (beta 8), cutoff at the
  original Nyquist, each branch normalised to unit DC gain. Detector delay 8 samples, inside the
  lookahead. Interpolation accuracy: within 0.07 dB of the exact 4x grid up to 18 kHz.
- Gain: required gain from the detected peak, sliding-window minimum (monotonic deque), release
  one-pole (100 ms, instant down), then a boxcar (attack). The first version used one window
  length for hold and boxcar; it kept every sample under the ceiling but a clipped sweep's
  4x-oversampled peak overshot by 1 dB, because the gain ramped inside the interpolator's support.
  Fixed by holding the gain flat across +-8 samples of every peak: hold H = L + 1 = 97 samples,
  boxcar B = L - 15 = 81 samples (proof in the file header). Now the 4x peak holds at the ceiling
  (-3.00 dBFS on every window of the sweep).
- The ceiling is a k-rate AudioParam ('ceilingDbfs') the host moves with setTargetAtTime (50 ms);
  inside the worklet a 5 ms one-pole removes the per-quantum steps. The Safety limiter is the same
  processor with a fixed ceiling (the parameter is ignored).
- The sample counter is rebased every 2^30 samples (about 6 hours) so bitwise ring indexing never
  overflows in a long live session.
- Worklet code is TypeScript compiled to one module by Vite ('?worker&url'). Checked with a
  production build: the worklet module is self-contained (9.6 KB, no imports). Hosts that do not
  use Vite pass GraphIo.workletModuleUrl. AudioWorkletGlobalScope has no TypeScript lib, so
  src/worklets/scope.d.ts declares the globals used and tsconfig.worklet.json compiles against it.

Tone generator
- Each warble and the calibration ramp go through their own Safety limiter instance (RULES: the
  Safety limiter is the last node on every path). Tones never exceed -6 dBFS, so it never acts; it
  adds its 2 ms lookahead, which the returned onsetTime includes.
- Calibration ramp: 32 steps of 1.5 dB from -60 to -12 dBFS, each a 5 ms linear ramp scheduled
  ahead (no setTargetAtTime); holds -12 dBFS for one step, then fades out. levelAt(t) reports the
  level heard at audio time t; stop() fades out and returns it.

Offline renderer
- Each segment's input runs latencySamples past its 10 ms tail. The first version stopped the input
  at the tail and the limiters looked ahead into silence there, so joins differed from a single pass
  (-39 dB locally at one join). Fixed; segmented output is now bit-identical to a single pass in
  practice (-348 dB overall, -327 dB at each join), so there are no compressor-state differences to
  document. The first segment has no pre-roll (it starts from silence exactly as a single pass).
- A MessagePort with an onmessage handler stays reachable while entangled, and the handler closed
  over each segment's graph, context and buffers: memory grew by about 45 MB per segment (961 MB
  after 30 minutes). dispose() now clears and closes every worklet port. After the fix, retained
  memory is flat.
- The renderer drops source data as soon as it is copied into the segment's AudioBuffer, keeping
  only the next segment's pre-roll.
- Cancellation: AbortSignal is checked before each segment and every 10 s of render time (via
  ctx.suspend); an aborted render rejects with AbortError and its context is dropped.
- MEMORY TEST APPROACH: Playwright in Chromium, not Node. A Web Audio polyfill exists
  (node-web-audio-api 2.2.0, BSD-3-Clause, actively maintained), but Chromium is the engine the app
  ships on, and the polyfill would be a new dependency with its own AudioWorklet and compressor
  behaviour. The test samples, every second, the page heap from CDP Runtime.getHeapUsage (usedSize
  plus backingStorageSize, which includes ArrayBuffer stores) before and after a forced garbage
  collection (HeapProfiler.collectGarbage), and the renderer process's RSS from `ps`. The bound
  applies to retained memory: peak below 300 MB and no growth (median of the second half within
  10 percent of the first). Measured on 30 minutes (15 segments): retained peak 100 to 190 MB
  including a 52 MB page baseline; raw peak (uncollected garbage) 234 MB; renderer RSS peak 273 to
  304 MB including Chromium itself. RSS gets only an absolute bound (500 MB), because it moves with
  the OS and with parallel tests.

Discontinuity detector
- Second difference against a robust scale (median absolute deviation x 1.4826, 2048-sample
  centred window, recomputed every 256 samples), threshold 6 robust SDs, absolute floor 1e-4,
  events merged within 5 ms. The threshold is measured, not guessed: clean renders of the
  programme signal through every preset reach 3.7 at most. The first threshold tried, 12, missed
  even a -20 dBFS click on processed programme.
- Digital silence: the median is computed over non-silent samples, because a window that is mostly
  silent (end of a fade) has a median of zero and made the fade's own curvature look infinite. In
  real silence the absolute floor alone applies, so a lone spike there is still found.
- Measured sensitivity on processed programme (output peak -12 dBFS): instantaneous gain steps of
  3 dB and more, and clicks of -26 dBFS and more. Smaller steps (1 to 2 dB) are not detectable by
  this method on dense programme. Any control applied without smoothing (preset, mode, 24 dB EQ
  jump) steps the level by more than 3 dB. The click test also asserts that the input itself has no
  detections and that injected faults are found (positive control).
- The programme test signal's pink-noise bed is -44 dBFS RMS: at -28 dBFS the EQ's high-frequency
  boost turned it into broadband noise that hid every fault from a second-difference detector.

Performance probe
- AudioWorkletGlobalScope in Chromium 158 has no `performance` object (checked 2026-11-09 by
  listing the global scope); only Date.now (1 ms). AudioContext.renderCapacity is not present. Each
  worklet therefore records the mean of quantised Date.now differences per process() call, which is
  an unbiased estimate of the mean over many calls because quanta are not locked to the clock's
  ticks; the maximum is not meaningful. performance.now is used automatically where it exists.
  graph.getPerf() returns every worklet's report. The Playwright probe also measures offline
  throughput (graph versus passthrough). Numbers in docs/perf.txt (rewritten on every e2e run):
  live worklet sum 0.8 to 2.9 percent of a 2.67 ms quantum on an Apple M3 (budget 15 percent with
  cleanup on); offline whole graph about 25 to 36 times real time.

M06 (2026-11-09): Night mode and Background cleanup

Night mode
- Rider (dsp/rider.ts, worklet clearly-rider): BS.1770 K-weighting with the 48 kHz table
  constants (Table 1 pre-filter, Table 2 RLB; analogue-prototype formulas at other rates),
  short-term loudness over 3 s from 100 ms blocks (L and R mean squares summed, -0.691 offset),
  target gain clamped to +boost/-cut, slewed per sample at most +3 dB/s and -6 dB/s; when the
  3 s loudness is below -60 LUFS the target freezes at the current gain (the gate). Zero latency.
- Levels map to core's NIGHT_MODE_LEVELS (low = Light, medium = Medium, high = Strong).
  Compressors after the rider (constants.ts NIGHT_LEVELS), each threshold 6 dB under the level's
  target so it catches only peaks the slow rider lets through:
    Light  -30 dBFS, knee 10 dB, ratio 3, attack 10 ms, release 250 ms (natural, gentle).
    Medium -32 dBFS, knee 8 dB, ratio 5, attack 6 ms, release 200 ms.
    Strong -34 dBFS, knee 6 dB, ratio 8, attack 3 ms, release 150 ms (holds loud scenes firmly).
- The compressor slot runs the normal stage and the Night stage in parallel and crossfades them
  (30 ms time constant). Both are DynamicsCompressorNodes and the rider adds no delay, so the
  latency budget and the dry delay are unchanged (all M02 latency and null tests still pass).
  Cost: the Night branch always runs (rider about 1 to 19 us per quantum).
- Measured (Playwright, 48 s programme alternating 8 s passages 30 dB apart, last 4 s of each):
  loudness range shrinks by at least 6 / 9 / 12 dB at Light / Medium / Strong, strictly more per
  level; peaks stay at or under -3 dBFS sample and -2.5 dBFS 4x-oversampled; no clicks while
  switching levels and off (discontinuity detector on both channels). An earlier version of the
  click test passed one channel where the detector expects a list of channels and so checked
  nothing; fixed before recording the result.
- Choosing a preset clears a remembered Night on/off choice, so the Night chip always turns Night
  on and other presets follow their own Night setting. (Without this, a remembered "off" beat the
  Night preset under core's precedence and the chip did nothing.)

- No active noise cancellation was built (the M09 prompt removes the Hum Canceller).
- Hum Filter detection (packages/audio dsp/humDetect.ts, pure): Tap A AnalyserNode, 8192 FFT,
  Blackman, smoothing 0, every 500 ms; spectra averaged over 3 s in linear power (speech moves,
  hum does not); parabolic interpolation; 12 dB above the local median; lock after 3 s steady
  within 0.5 Hz; follow the median of recent frames (30 percent per update); 2 s hold after any
  change; frames where programme hides the hum do not reset the history (released only after 6 s
  with no hum at all).
- Hum notches: a single Q-30 notch removes 25 dB only within about +-0.04 Hz of the hum, which an
  8192-point FFT cannot pin under speech. Each frequency gets two stagger-tuned Q-30 notches
  (+-0.12 percent), so HUM_MAX_NOTCHES is 12 (six frequencies). Once locked, the fundamental and
  all five harmonics are notched. Measured: hum at 49.5, 50.5, 59.5 and 60.5 Hz detected within
  0.2 Hz, at least 25 dB lower, speech band (300-3400 Hz) changed under 1 dB; mode switches click-
  free. The prompt's "frequency drift of +-0.5 Hz" is tested as hum offset by +-0.5 Hz from 50 and
  60; a hum that keeps sliding is followed slowly by the tracker (not render-tested).
