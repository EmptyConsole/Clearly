CLEARLY: MANUAL TESTS
Checks that need real hardware, real browsers or a person. Run before each release
(RELEASE_CHECKLIST.txt) and record the date, browser and version, OS and result next to each.

M05: CLEAR AUDIO
1. Tab capture, Chrome (latest), Windows, macOS and Linux
   a. Open a video in one tab and Clearly in another. Clear Audio, "Choose a tab", follow the
      coach, pick the video tab, turn on "Also share tab audio".
   b. Expect: the chip "Enhancing: <tab title>", the line "Switch to your video now...", sound
      enhanced; the original tab is silent (suppressLocalAudioPlayback).
   c. Switch to the video tab for 5 minutes. Expect: no gaps or slowdowns. Note any throttling.
   d. Stop sharing from the browser's bar. Expect: "Sharing stopped..." and a way to choose again.
   e. Cancel the picker. Expect: nothing happens, no error.
   f. Share a tab without ticking audio. Expect: "No sound was shared..." with Try again.
2. Tab capture, Edge (latest): repeat 1a to 1f.
3. suppressLocalAudioPlayback per OS: on Windows, macOS and Linux, record whether the original
   tab is silenced. Where it is not, expect the "You may hear the sound twice" warning; try "Mute
   the original tab..." and answer Yes or No; record what happens to Clearly's own sound.
4. Output device (setSinkId): with two outputs connected, choose the second in Advanced. Expect
   sound to move; unplug it and record what happens (M14 fault-injection covers the app's
   response).
5. Long file export: export a 2-hour MP4 to MP3 with the save picker. Record time and the
   renderer's memory (Task Manager); expect a steady level.

M07: CAPTIONS
1. Offline captions: download Standard captions, then turn off networking (or stop the server)
   and reload the app from cache (PWA, M10) or keep the tab open; open a file and turn captions
   on. Expect captions with no network.
2. Document Picture-in-Picture (Chrome, Edge): play a file with captions on, press "Open
   floating captions", then switch to another window or a video tab. Expect the small window to
   stay on top with captions in the chosen size and colours; Escape inside it closes it; a screen
   reader announces new captions.
3. Long file: open a 45-minute or longer video, turn on captions with Captions first. Expect
   "Captions ready for X of Y" to climb, playback to wait until 10 s are ready, and reopening the
   same file later to show captions at once.
4. Live captions on a browser tab (M05 tab capture): expect the one-time "Live captions lag a few
   seconds behind the picture" line, each caption on screen at least 6 s, and "Captions skipped
   ahead to keep up" on a slow computer with More accurate.
5. WebGPU: on a machine with a GPU (not headless), check the Captions card says "Using the
   graphics processor" in Chrome; record browser and GPU.

M09 QUIET LAB
1. Auto Boost in a real noisy room: wear headphones, play a file in Clear Audio, turn on Auto
   Boost (headphones step, microphone pre-prompt, browser permission). Run a kettle, a fan or a
   TV in the room. Expect the room word to rise (Quiet, A little noisy, Noisy, Very noisy) over a
   couple of seconds, "Currently adding +X dB" to climb towards +6 dB, and voices to sound a
   little clearer; when the noise stops, expect it to settle back over about 5 seconds with no
   fluttering.
2. Feedback guard with speakers: unplug the headphones so the show plays through the laptop
   speakers, with Auto Boost on. Expect "Auto Boost paused: it can hear your show. Are you using
   speakers?" within about 15 seconds, and the boost to stop changing. Plug headphones back in and
   press "I am on headphones now, resume".
3. Microphone denied: block the microphone when the browser asks. Expect the "The microphone is
   blocked" panel with how to allow it again and Try again; Clear Audio keeps playing.
4. Hum Filter on a real humming video (for example an old home video or a recording with a
   ground-loop hum): open it in Clear Audio, set Hum Filter to Auto, press Play. Expect "Removing
   hum at 50 Hz and 5 harmonics" (or 60) within about 5 seconds and the hum to be much quieter
   while voices sound the same. Switch Off / 50 Hz / 60 Hz during playback: no clicks.
5. Calm Sounds on a phone: play Rain, lock the screen. Note whether sound continues (it may
   pause; the card says so). Check the 15-minute sleep timer fades out smoothly over the last
   minute.
6. Pitch match with real headphones: the first tone should be soft; Higher and Lower move in small
   steps; the saved pitch appears; "Matched to my ringing" plays a noise with a gap at that pitch.

M10 SCREEN READERS, TIMEOUTS, THEMES
VoiceOver (macOS Safari and iOS Safari):
1. Rotor > Landmarks lists one "Main" navigation and the main region on every page.
2. Each route change announces the new page heading (focus moves to the h1).
3. Hearing Check stage: the status line announces each step; the big button is announced as a
   button with its label; Space and Enter respond.
4. Charts (audiogram, tracker, Insight): "Show the table" exposes a table with headers.
5. Clear Audio: Original | Enhanced is a radio group; "Now hearing" is announced.
6. Floating captions (Chrome only; with VoiceOver in Chrome): new captions are announced.
NVDA (Windows, Firefox and Chrome):
7. Browse mode reads each page in order; form mode works in Settings fields.
8. Dialogs (headphones, delete profile) trap focus and Escape closes them.
9. Toasts are announced once (role status) and do not steal focus.
Timeouts:
10. Leave each prompt (pre-check, calibration, mic pre-prompt, install card, update card,
    dialogs) for at least 20 seconds: nothing disappears or advances by itself (Hearing Check
    tones excepted).
Themes and motion:
11. Settings > Appearance: Light, Dark, High contrast and Match my device change every screen at
    once; with the device's Reduce motion on, no transitions play.
12. Text size 200 percent (browser zoom or OS text size): no page scrolls sideways; wide tables
    scroll inside their own box.
PWA:
13. Production build on a phone: install from the browser menu (or Add to Home Screen), open it
    with networking off: the app shell, Hearing Check, demo and Quiet Lab work; downloads and
    backup say they need a connection.
14. Deploy a new build while the installed app is open: "A new version is ready" appears and
    nothing reloads until Reload is pressed.

M11 VIDEO EXPORT
1. A real one-hour video (H.264 + AAC MP4, for example a screen recording or a phone video):
   Clear Audio > Save enhanced copy > Video with enhanced sound > Download the video tool >
   Make the copy. The estimate is roughly right; the progress bar moves through "Step 1 of 2"
   and "Step 2 of 2"; Save writes "<name> (Clearly).mp4". The result plays in the system player
   with the picture unchanged, the enhanced sound in sync from start to end, and the original
   title and chapters.
2. A file just under the 1 GB cap (for example 990 MB): video export is offered and finishes;
   browser memory (Activity Monitor or Task Manager) stays under about 3 GB. A file just over
   1 GB: no video option, and the sentence about the 1 GB limit shows; WAV and MP3 still work.
3. A WebM (VP9) screen recording becomes an MP4; an old AVI with an MPEG-4 Part 2 video becomes
   an MP4; a WebM with VP8 becomes an MKV. Each plays in VLC.
4. An MKV with SubRip subtitles becomes an MP4 whose subtitles show in QuickTime or VLC; an MKV
   with Blu-ray (PGS) subtitles says that one subtitle track was left out.
5. Cancel during each step: "Cancelled. Nothing was saved."; in DevTools > Application >
   Storage, the Origin Private File System has no clearly-export-tmp files left.
6. A private (incognito) window: video export still works (the temporary sound goes to memory
   instead of the small private storage).
7. A phone (or DevTools device mode with touch): no video option, and the sentence explains why.
8. The download step's "Its licence and source" link opens a page with the FFmpeg tarball, the
   patch, the build script and the LGPL text.

M12 DESKTOP APP
1. Windows 11, system sound: Clear Audio > All computer sound > Start; play a video in another
   app: Clearly enhances it; Original | Enhanced changes it. Record whether you hear the
   original too (expected: yes, and the warning offers the cable).
2. Windows, loopbackWithMute: build with audio 'loopbackWithMute' in capture.ts and repeat 1.
   Does the original go silent while Clearly's own sound still plays? Only if yes may it be
   turned on (record the result in DECISIONS).
3. macOS: All computer sound is not offered; the cable card says it is the way on this computer.
   Linux: the same (record the distribution and result).
4. Virtual cable with BlackHole 2ch (macOS) and with VB-CABLE (Windows): install it, follow the
   four steps; the cable is marked "cable found"; choosing it on both sides is refused; Test the
   routing passes within 2 seconds; Start plays the video app's sound through Clearly only.
5. Tray: Enhanced and Original switch the open player; Show Clearly brings the window back;
   Quit quits.
6. Global shortcut: Command/Ctrl+Shift+E toggles from another app; change it in Settings >
   Desktop app; pick one another app already uses: Settings says it could not be used; turn it
   off: it stops working.
7. Floating captions across two displays: open them, move them to the second display, close
   with Escape, reopen: they come back on that display at that size; screen reader reads new
   lines.
8. Automatic backup: change a setting; within 2 seconds profile-backup.json in the user-data
   folder changes; Show backup folder opens it; kill the app during a write: the file is never
   half-written.
9. Unsigned first launch on macOS and Windows following docs/DESKTOP.txt; confirm the macOS
   wording of "Open Anyway" for the current macOS version and update DESKTOP.txt.

M14 PHONES AND TABLETS (real devices; record device, OS and browser versions)
1. iPhone, Safari, installed to the Home Screen: Quiet Lab > Calm Sounds, play Pink noise, lock the
   screen. Does it keep playing for 5 minutes? Do the lock-screen controls show "Calm Sounds" and
   does pause stop it? Record the result in MOBILE.txt; only then may the Calm Sounds card change.
2. Android, Chrome, installed: the same as 1.
3. Safe areas: on a phone with a notch or home indicator, the tab bar sits above the indicator in
   portrait and landscape; nothing hides under the notch.
4. Hearing Check on a phone: the button is easy to hit with a thumb; the screen does not dim or
   lock during the check (wake lock); each tap gives a short vibration (Android).
5. The silent switch (iPhone): with the ring/silent switch on silent, the Hearing Check tones and
   Clear Audio still play through headphones.
6. A file from the phone's Files app or gallery opens (audio and video).
7. Media Session: while Clear Audio plays, the lock screen shows the title, and play/pause work.
8. Mobile data: with Wi-Fi off, the captions and Background cleanup downloads ask before starting.
9. Export: a 31-minute file says it is too long; a 20-minute file saves as WAV; no video option.
10. The phone page's QR code opens the right address from another phone's camera.

HEARING CHECK WITH COMPUTER SPEAKERS
1. A laptop's built-in speakers, then a pair of desk speakers: choose Computer speakers, sit at
   arm's length, set a comfortable level, run Express. Every beep comes from both speakers; the
   screen says "Both ears (speakers)"; it takes less time than with headphones; the results say
   both ears were checked together; the PDF says "Played through computer speakers".
2. Compare with a headphone check on the same day: the speakers result should be in the same
   region (expect several dB of difference from the room and speakers). Record the difference.
3. Fine-tune (Refine) after a speakers check: it stays in speakers mode; pausing and resuming
   keeps it.
